# The exemplar — what a finished complaint looks like

This is the standard the skill writes to. Read it before drafting and match its register exactly: **third person** ("Student", "the Parent", "the District"), **nearly every sentence carrying a date, a number, a document, or a specific act**, no legal conclusions in the facts, regulations under the headings only, and **dense** — each fact once, nothing characterised, nothing computed. Density is the standard, not length.

The people, district and facts below are invented. Use nothing from it but its voice. The printed form — the caption, the numbered paragraphs, the closing, the certificate — is the assembler's and is described in `format.md`; the two worked examples in `examples/` show it as rendered.

## I. Introduction

1. Petitioner Jordan Rivera ("Student") is an 8-year-old student who has been eligible for special education and related services under the category of Autism since March 11, 2024, and who attends Willow Creek Elementary School in the River Oak Unified School District ("District"). This due process complaint is brought on Student's behalf by the parent, Dana Rivera ("Parent"), who is self-represented.

2. The District has denied Student a free appropriate public education ("FAPE") by failing to provide an individualized education program ("IEP") adequate to Student's needs; and by failing to implement the services the operative IEP requires.

## II. Contact and residence information

*34 C.F.R. § 300.508(b)(1)–(3).*

3. Student was born on November 3, 2017, resides with the Parent at 1418 Alder Street, Willow Creek, CA 95833, and is enrolled at Willow Creek Elementary School. The Parent may be reached at dana.r@example.com and (555) 010-4471.

## III. Statement of facts

4. On September 8, 2025, the IEP team adopted an annual reading goal of 60 words per minute with 90% accuracy, carried forward from the March 11, 2024 IEP.

5. On October 6, 2025, the small-group reading block was discontinued when the reading intervention position became vacant.

6. On November 14, 2025, the District's progress report recorded 21 words per minute.

7. On January 23, 2026, the District's progress report recorded 24 words per minute.

8. On March 6, 2026, the District's progress report recorded 23 words per minute and stated that the goal was not met.

9. On March 12, 2026, the IEP team declined to revise the reading goal, and the District issued a prior written notice.

10. On March 14, 2026, the Parent requested revision of the goal in writing.

## IV. Statement of the problems

### A. Failure to provide an adequate individualized education program

*34 C.F.R. §§ 300.320, 300.324.*

11. The IEP dated September 8, 2025 provides an annual reading goal that Student "will increase oral reading fluency to 60 words per minute with 90% accuracy." The IEP dated March 11, 2024 had set the same goal at 60 words per minute.

12. The District's progress reports dated November 14, 2025, January 23, 2026 and March 6, 2026 recorded 21, 24 and 23 words per minute, and the March 6, 2026 report states that the goal was "not met."

13. On March 12, 2026, the IEP team declined to revise the goal; the prior written notice of that date gives as its reason that the goal "remains appropriate." The Parent requested revision in writing on March 14, 2026, and on April 2, 2026 wrote that she had received no response.

### B. Failure to implement the services the IEP requires

*34 C.F.R. § 300.323.*

14. The IEP dated September 8, 2025 provides 240 minutes per week of specialized academic instruction in reading in a small-group setting.

15. The District's service delivery log for September 8, 2025 to February 27, 2026 records 24 instructional weeks, 4 weeks at the full 240 minutes, an average of 95 minutes per week, and 20 weeks below 240 minutes. It notes that the small-group reading block was discontinued on October 6, 2025 when the reading intervention position became vacant.

16. The prior written notice of March 12, 2026 states that the District "acknowledges the reduction in small-group reading instruction since October 2025" and offers no compensatory services.

## V. Proposed resolution

*34 C.F.R. § 300.508(b)(6).*

17. The Parent proposes the following resolution:

    (a) convene an IEP team meeting within 15 days to adopt measurable annual reading goals based on current assessment data;

    (b) fund an independent educational evaluation in reading and written expression;

    (c) provide compensatory specialized academic instruction equal to the minutes not delivered between October 6, 2025 and February 27, 2026; and

    (d) implement the IEP as written, with quarterly service logs provided to the Parent.

## What to notice

- **Every paragraph of the statement is a fact with a date or a figure.** "On September 8, 2025, the IEP team adopted…", "21, 24 and 23 words per minute", "an average of 95 minutes per week", "20 weeks below 240 minutes". Nothing is characterised and nothing is computed: not "a shortfall of 145 minutes per week", not "less than half the minutes", not "no measurable progress" — the gate refuses each of those, because no document states them (`drafting-rules.md`, rule 2). State the figures the documents give and let the reader compare.
- **The statement of facts (III) is the chronology**, one dated event per paragraph, oldest first, each written from a document. **The statement of the problems (IV)** gathers, under each claim, the facts that bear on it; it may restate a dated event from III, and the gate allows that.
- **The documents are named and dated in the prose** ("The District's progress reports dated November 14, 2025, January 23, 2026 and March 6, 2026"). There are no page cites in the text; the page lives in sources.md, for the person checking.
- **A quotation is short and exact**, and only where the document's own words are the fact (the goal's wording, the notice's reason).
- **Nothing concludes.** "The Parent requested revision in writing on March 14, 2026, and on April 2, 2026 wrote that she had received no response." — not "in violation of its obligations". The regulation under each heading does that work.
- **The introduction is the one place a standard is named**: "denied Student a free appropriate public education ("FAPE") by …" — and the clauses after "by" are the fixed wording of the claims chosen (`references/claims.json`), never composed.
- **The relief is lettered, concrete, and within what a hearing officer can order.** On a represented filing, counsel's reservation of the right to seek attorneys' fees follows the lettered list as its own paragraph.
- **When counsel signs**, the signature block carries the attorney's name, "Attorney for Petitioner", bar number, firm and address, and "Self-represented (pro se)" appears nowhere. The certificate of service is then signed by counsel.
