# The claims, and what each one turns on

Read this before you recommend claims (step 4). It is how you decide which claims the file
supports. It is never shown to the person as a list.

A claim is **recommended** when the documents, or the person's own words, give a fact for **each
required part** below, and for no other reason. The number is whatever that gives: none, one, or all
of them. **Raise every claim the record supports.** An issue left out of the complaint may be barred
at the hearing, so leaving one out to keep the list short is the costlier mistake. The person still
chooses (rule 3).

Never write *strong*, *weak*, *likely*, *best*, *viable*, *merit*, *score*, *rank* or *win* about a
claim, in the complaint or in chat. You are saying what the documents support, not how the case
will fare.

## What counts as a fact for a part

- **A part is carried by something that happened and is recorded:** a refusal, a statement, a
  figure, an entry in a log, a date, the IEP's own words. Point to it: the document and page, or the
  person's words in `statement.md`.
- **A document that states an omission is a fact. Only silence is not.** A letter saying the plan
  never arrived, a log recording sessions not delivered, an IEP whose list of services leaves the
  service out, a prior written notice refusing something: each is a record of the thing that did not
  happen, and each carries a part. What carries nothing is **silence**: a request nobody answered,
  with no document recording the non-answer. Where the only support for a part is the person saying
  they never got something, write the claim with **[MISSING-#]** on that part rather than leaving the
  claim out. The record may hold what they have not seen.
- **A part about what the District did is carried only by what the District did or said.** An
  evaluator's recommendation, a doctor's letter or the Parent's request is not a District decision,
  offer or refusal. It carries the *need*; the District's answer carries the *refusal*.
- **The IEP's list of services is the District's offer.** A service, support or device the IEP does
  not list was not offered in it.
- **Count; don't estimate.** A deadline is measured from the dates on the documents, and a person's
  age from the date of birth against the dates of the IEP's whole term, not only its meeting date.
  Every count goes in the arithmetic table (`references/audit.md` § 2) with its inputs.
- **One claim per heading below.** Refusals of different services are different claims, each with
  its own regulation and remedy. Don't merge them to shorten the list, and don't split one into two
  to lengthen it.
- **Answer the District's own reasons.** Where the record holds the District's explanation for what
  it did — a prior written notice, an email, a meeting note — the claim answers it. See
  `references/exemplar.md`, "What to notice".

## The claims

Each claim gives its heading as the complaint prints it, then the regulation in italics as it goes
under the heading. Cite only the subsections a claim actually turns on; never guess a subsection
number, and flag any you have not confirmed against the official text with **[VERIFY-#]**.

### 1. Failure to provide a free appropriate public education

*34 C.F.R. §§ 300.101, 300.320, 300.324*

- Required: what the student needs, as an evaluation, a report or the IEP's own present levels
  describe it.
- Required: where the IEP as written falls short of it. That is a recommendation the IEP does not
  carry, a goal repeated unchanged or not measurable, or reported lack of progress under it.

### 2. Failure to educate Student alongside children without disabilities

*34 C.F.R. §§ 300.114–300.120*

- Required: a decision or proposal by the District placing the student in a more separate setting.
- Required: a fact that the less separate setting was possible or was not considered. That could be
  an evaluation or provider recommending it with supports, supplementary aids and services not
  considered, or a request for it refused. Removal from the regular class is allowed only when
  education there with supplementary aids and services cannot be achieved satisfactorily
  (§ 300.114(a)(2)(ii)), and the District must make a continuum of alternative placements available
  (§ 300.115).

### 3. Failure to evaluate appropriately and on time

*34 C.F.R. §§ 300.111, 300.301–300.311*

- Required: a trigger. That is a request for an evaluation (a parent may request an initial
  evaluation, § 300.301(b)), consent to one, or a documented reason to suspect a disability; child
  find reaches a child suspected of a disability "even though they are advancing from grade to
  grade" (§ 300.111(c)(1)).
- Required: what the District did instead:
  - a refusal;
  - no initial evaluation within 60 days of receiving consent, or the state's own timeframe
    (§ 300.301(c)(1));
  - an area of need not assessed — the evaluation must assess the child "in all areas related to the
    suspected disability" (§ 300.304(c)(4)) and be "sufficiently comprehensive to identify all of
    the child's special education and related services needs" (§ 300.304(c)(6));
  - or no reevaluation in three years, or a request for one refused (§ 300.303) — though a request
    made less than a year after the last one may be refused unless the District agrees
    (§ 300.303(b)(1)).
- A request for a functional behavior assessment of one student is generally treated as a request
  for an evaluation.

### 4. Refusal to fund an independent educational evaluation

*34 C.F.R. § 300.502*

- Required: the Parent disagreed with an evaluation the District obtained, and asked for an
  independent educational evaluation at public expense, with the date of the request
  (§ 300.502(b)(1)).
- Required: the District neither funded it nor filed a due process complaint to show its own
  evaluation was appropriate "without unnecessary delay" (§ 300.502(b)(2)).
- Where the Parent already obtained the evaluation and seeks reimbursement, the request is for
  reimbursement under § 300.502(b)(3) and the amount comes from the invoice in the record.

### 5. Denial of parent participation, including predetermination

*34 C.F.R. §§ 300.322, 300.501(b)–(c), 300.513(a)(2)*

- Required: a step that gives the Parent a part in the decision, missed or defective: no notice of
  the meeting, the meeting held at a time or place not mutually agreed, the Parent not afforded the
  opportunity to take part (§ 300.322), or the Parent excluded from the placement decision
  (§ 300.501(c)).
- Required for predetermination specifically: a record fact that the decision was made before the
  meeting — a completed IEP presented as final, a refusal to consider a recommendation on the table,
  a written statement that the program was already set, or a proposal unchanged after the Parent's
  evidence.
- The effect: see *Procedural claims* below.

### 6. Failure to reconvene or revise the IEP

*34 C.F.R. § 300.324(b)*

- Required: a fact putting the District on notice that the IEP was not working — a progress report
  recording no or insufficient progress, a provider's report, a Parent request to reconvene, an
  evaluation.
- Required: the District did not revise the IEP, or did not convene the team, after it. The team
  must revise the IEP as appropriate to address "any lack of expected progress toward the annual
  goals" (§ 300.324(b)(1)(ii)(A)).

### 7. Failure to give prior written notice

*34 C.F.R. § 300.503*

- Required: the District proposed or refused to initiate or change the identification, evaluation,
  or educational placement of the child, or the provision of FAPE (§ 300.503(a)).
- Required: no notice was given a reasonable time before, or the notice given lacks a required
  content element (§ 300.503(b)) — a description of the action, an explanation of why, the other
  options considered and why they were rejected.
- The effect: see *Procedural claims* below.

### 8. Failure to implement the services the IEP requires

*34 C.F.R. § 300.323*

- Required: what the IEP requires, meaning a stated service, amount or support.
- Required: a record that it was not delivered as the IEP states, such as a log, a notice or the
  District's own account. A person's report of what a provider or teacher told them counts, with its
  date. The District must make the services available "in accordance with the child's IEP"
  (§ 300.323(c)(2)).
- A staffing vacancy, an unfilled position, a provider absence with no make-up, and a group
  delivered where the IEP says individual each carry the second part.

### 9. Failure to provide required related services

*34 C.F.R. § 300.34*

- Required: a document identifying a need for a related service, such as speech-language therapy,
  counseling, occupational therapy or transportation.
- Required: the IEP does not provide it, provides less than was recommended, or the District refused
  it.

### 10. Failure to address Student's social, emotional and behavioral needs

*34 C.F.R. §§ 300.34(c)(2), 300.324(a)(2)(i)*

- Required: a documented social, emotional or behavioral need — an evaluation, a counselor's or
  teacher's report, a discipline record, the IEP's own present levels.
- Required: the IEP provides no counseling or behavioral support for it, or the team did not
  consider positive behavioral interventions and supports where the behavior impedes the child's
  learning or that of others (§ 300.324(a)(2)(i)).

### 11. Failure to provide required assistive technology

*34 C.F.R. § 300.105*

- Required: a documented need for assistive technology, or a request to evaluate for it.
- Required: the District did not consider, evaluate or provide it. The IEP team must consider
  whether the student needs it (§ 300.324(a)(2)(v)).
- Where the record shows the device is needed at home for the student to receive FAPE, the District
  must permit home use on a case-by-case basis (§ 300.105(b)).

### 12. Failure to provide extended school year services

*34 C.F.R. § 300.106*

- Required: regression over breaks from school, or a recommendation for extended school year
  services.
- Required: the District refused, did not offer, or did not consider them. They must be available
  when the IEP team determines, individually, that they are necessary for FAPE, and may not be
  limited to particular categories of disability or unilaterally limited in type, amount or duration
  (§ 300.106(a)).

### 13. Failure to provide required transition services

*34 C.F.R. §§ 300.43, 300.320(b)*

- Required: the student's age. Transition goals and services are required "not later than the first
  IEP to be in effect when the child turns 16" (§ 300.320(b)). Count the birthday against the IEP's
  whole term: an IEP written at 15 that runs past the 16th birthday must have them. Some states set
  a younger age; where the state's rules say so, use it.
- Required: the IEP lacks measurable postsecondary goals based on age-appropriate transition
  assessments, or the transition services (including courses of study) needed to reach them.

### 14. Removal from school without the required protections

*34 C.F.R. §§ 300.530–300.536*

- Required: a suspension, expulsion or other removal, with its dates.
- Required: the protection that failed:
  - a change of placement (more than 10 consecutive school days, or a series of removals totalling
    more than 10 school days in a school year that is a pattern, § 300.536) with no manifestation
    determination within 10 school days of the decision (§ 300.530(e));
  - no services during a removal that required them (§ 300.530(d));
  - or a placement change made without the required review.
- A removal of 10 school days or fewer, standing alone, carries neither part.
- Discipline hearings are expedited; say so in the filing instructions.

### 15. Failure to provide access to education records

*34 C.F.R. §§ 300.613, 300.501(a), 300.513(a)(2)*

- Required: a request to inspect and review the student's education records, with its date.
- Required: a refusal, or a response later than the rules allow. The District must comply "without
  unnecessary delay and before any meeting regarding an IEP, or any hearing pursuant to § 300.507 or
  §§ 300.530 through 300.532, or resolution session pursuant to § 300.510, and in no case more than
  45 days after the request has been made" (§ 300.613(a)).
- Count the days between the dated request and the dated reply, in the arithmetic table.
- The federal right is to inspect and review. It includes copies only where not providing them
  "would effectively prevent" that (§ 300.613(b)(2)). Some states give a right to copies and a
  shorter deadline; where the state's rules say so, use them.
- The effect: see *Procedural claims* below.

### 16. Cumulative procedural violations

*34 C.F.R. § 300.513(a)(2)*

- Required: two or more procedural failures pleaded elsewhere in the complaint, each carried by its
  own record fact.
- Required: the effect, under *Procedural claims* below, of the failures taken together.
- Plead this only alongside the procedural claims it draws on, and point to their paragraphs rather
  than restating them.

### 17. Reimbursement for a unilateral placement

*34 C.F.R. § 300.148*

**Recommend this only where the person has confirmed a unilateral placement.** Never infer one from
a private school's name in the file.

- Required: the person's confirmation that they enrolled the student in a private school or program
  at their own expense because they considered the District's program inadequate.
- Required: a fact that the District's program was inadequate — ordinarily the facts already
  pleaded under claim 1.
- Required: what the placement provides, from its own records, enrolment letter or invoices.
- Required: the notice. Reimbursement may be reduced or denied where the Parent did not tell the IEP
  team of their concerns and intent at the most recent meeting before the removal, or in writing at
  least 10 business days before it (§ 300.148(d)(1)); the exceptions are at § 300.148(e). Ask for the
  date and the form of the notice, and flag **[MISSING-#]** where the record does not hold it.
- The amounts come from the invoices in the record, carried into the arithmetic table.

### 18. Any other claim the hearing officer can decide

A parent may file a due process complaint "on any matter relating to the identification,
evaluation, or educational placement of a child with a disability, or the provision of FAPE to the
child" (§ 300.507(a)(1)). Where the record supports something the seventeen headings above do not
reach, plead it as its own lettered claim with the rule it turns on.

**This is where a state-law claim goes.** Many states require more than federal law: shorter
timelines, staffing ratios, additional meeting or notice requirements, a right to copies of records.
`references/authorities.md` § 5 says how to research the filing state's rules and how to cite them.
Plead any the record shows were violated, and flag **[VERIFY-#]** on any subsection you have not
confirmed against the state's own official text.

## Procedural claims: the effect

For the claims about procedure (parent participation, prior written notice, access to records, and
the cumulative claim), a hearing officer may find the student did not receive a FAPE "only if the
procedural inadequacies" (§ 300.513(a)(2)):

- impeded the child's right to a FAPE;
- significantly impeded the parent's opportunity to participate in decision-making about the
  provision of a FAPE;
- or caused a deprivation of educational benefit.

The file rarely records this, so it is not needed to recommend the claim. When the person chooses
one of these claims, ask them in their own words what it affected, and put the answer in
`statement.md`. Where they cannot say, plead the claim with **[MISSING-#]** on the effect.
