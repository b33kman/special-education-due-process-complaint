# The claims, and what each one turns on

Read this before you recommend claims (step 3). It is how you decide which claims the file supports; it is never shown to the person as a list.

A claim is **recommended** when the documents, or the person's own words, give a fact for **each required part** below, and for no other reason. The number is whatever that gives: none, one, or all eleven.

## What counts as a fact for a part

- **A part is carried by something that happened and is recorded:** a refusal, a statement, a figure, an entry in a log, a date, the IEP's own words. Point to it: the document and page, or the person's words in `statement.md`.
- **Something that did not happen is not a fact by itself.** A reply that never came, a notice the person never received, or a document that isn't in the file carries a part only when a document records it: a later letter saying the plan never arrived, a log recording sessions not delivered, or an IEP whose list of services leaves the service out. Where the only support for a part is the person saying they never got something, don't recommend the claim. The record may hold what they haven't seen. It stays on the list for them to add.
- **A part about what the District did is carried only by what the District did or said.** An evaluator's recommendation, a doctor's letter or the Parent's request is not a District decision, offer or refusal. It carries the *need*; the District's answer carries the *refusal*.
- **The IEP's list of services is the District's offer.** A service, support or device the IEP does not list was not offered in it.
- **Count; don't estimate.** A deadline is measured from the dates on the documents, and a person's age from the date of birth against the dates of the IEP's whole term, not only its meeting date.
- **One claim per heading below.** Refusals of different services are different claims, each with its own regulation and remedy. Don't merge them to shorten the list, and don't split one into two to lengthen it.

## The eleven claims

Each claim gives its heading as the complaint prints it, then the regulation in italics as it goes under the heading.

**Failure to provide an adequate individualized education program**, *34 C.F.R. §§ 300.320, 300.324*
- Required: what the student needs, as an evaluation, a report or the IEP's own present levels describe it.
- Required: where the IEP as written falls short of it. That is a recommendation the IEP does not carry, a goal repeated unchanged or not measurable, or reported lack of progress under it.

**Failure to implement the services the IEP requires**, *34 C.F.R. § 300.323*
- Required: what the IEP requires, meaning a stated service, amount or support.
- Required: a record that it was not delivered as the IEP states, such as a log, a notice or the District's own account. A person's report of what a provider or teacher told them counts, with its date. The District must make the services available "in accordance with the child's IEP" (§ 300.323(c)(2)).

**Failure to evaluate appropriately and on time**, *34 C.F.R. §§ 300.111, 300.301–300.311, 300.502*
- Required: a trigger. That is a request for an evaluation (a parent may request an initial evaluation, § 300.301(b)), consent to one, or a documented reason to suspect a disability; child find reaches a child suspected of a disability "even though they are advancing from grade to grade" (§ 300.111(c)(1)).
- Required: what the District did instead:
  - a refusal;
  - no initial evaluation within 60 days of receiving consent, or the state's own timeframe (§ 300.301(c)(1));
  - an area of need not assessed;
  - no reevaluation in three years, or a request for one refused (§ 300.303) — though a request made less than a year after the last one may be refused unless the District agrees (§ 300.303(b)(1));
  - or a request for an independent evaluation at public expense that the District neither funded nor defended by filing for a hearing "without unnecessary delay" (§ 300.502(b)(2)).
- A request for a functional behavior assessment of one student is generally treated as a request for an evaluation.

**Failure to follow required procedures**, *34 C.F.R. §§ 300.503, 300.322, 300.513(a)(2)*
- Required: a required step missed or defective. That includes:
  - no prior written notice when the District proposed or refused a change in identification, evaluation, placement or services (§ 300.503(a));
  - no notice of a meeting, or the Parent not afforded the opportunity to take part (§ 300.322);
  - a required member of the team missing.
- The effect: see *Procedural claims* below.

**Failure to educate Student alongside children without disabilities**, *34 C.F.R. §§ 300.114–300.120*
- Required: a decision or proposal by the District placing the student in a more separate setting.
- Required: a fact that the less separate setting was possible or was not considered. That could be an evaluation or provider recommending it with supports, supplementary aids and services not considered, or a request for it refused. Removal from the regular class is allowed only when education there with supplementary aids and services cannot be achieved satisfactorily (§ 300.114(a)(2)(ii)).

**Removal from school without the required protections**, *34 C.F.R. §§ 300.530–300.536*
- Required: a suspension, expulsion or other removal, with its dates.
- Required: the protection that failed:
  - a change of placement (more than 10 consecutive school days, or a series of removals totalling more than 10 school days in a school year that is a pattern, § 300.536) with no manifestation determination within 10 school days of the decision (§ 300.530(e));
  - no services during a removal that required them (§ 300.530(d));
  - or a placement change made without the required review.
- A removal of 10 school days or fewer, standing alone, carries neither part.
- Discipline hearings are expedited; say so in the filing instructions.

**Failure to provide required related services**, *34 C.F.R. § 300.34*
- Required: a document identifying a need for a related service, such as speech-language therapy, counseling, occupational therapy or transportation.
- Required: the IEP does not provide it, provides less than was recommended, or the District refused it.

**Failure to provide extended school year services**, *34 C.F.R. § 300.106*
- Required: regression over breaks from school, or a recommendation for extended school year services.
- Required: the District refused, did not offer, or did not consider them. They must be available when the IEP team determines, individually, that they are necessary for FAPE, and may not be limited to particular categories of disability or unilaterally limited in type, amount or duration (§ 300.106(a)).

**Failure to provide required assistive technology**, *34 C.F.R. § 300.105*
- Required: a documented need for assistive technology, or a request to evaluate for it.
- Required: the District did not consider, evaluate or provide it. The IEP team must consider whether the student needs it (§ 300.324(a)(2)(v)).

**Failure to provide required transition services**, *34 C.F.R. §§ 300.43, 300.320(b)*
- Required: the student's age. Transition goals and services are required "not later than the first IEP to be in effect when the child turns 16" (§ 300.320(b)). Count the birthday against the IEP's whole term: an IEP written at 15 that runs past the 16th birthday must have them. Some states set a younger age; where the state's rules say so, use it.
- Required: the IEP lacks measurable postsecondary goals based on age-appropriate transition assessments, or the transition services (including courses of study) needed to reach them.

**Failure to provide access to education records**, *34 C.F.R. §§ 300.613, 300.501(a), 300.513(a)(2)*
- Required: a request to inspect and review the student's education records, with its date.
- Required: a refusal, or a response later than the rules allow. The District must comply "without unnecessary delay and before any meeting regarding an IEP, or any hearing pursuant to § 300.507 or §§ 300.530 through 300.532, or resolution session pursuant to § 300.510, and in no case more than 45 days after the request has been made" (§ 300.613(a)).
- Count the days between the dated request and the dated reply.
- The federal right is to inspect and review. It includes copies only where not providing them "would effectively prevent" that (§ 300.613(b)(2)). Some states give a right to copies and a shorter deadline; where the state's rules say so, use them.
- The effect: see *Procedural claims* below.

## Procedural claims: the effect

For the two claims about procedure (required procedures, and access to records), a hearing officer may find the student did not receive a FAPE "only if the procedural inadequacies" (§ 300.513(a)(2)):
- impeded the child's right to a FAPE;
- significantly impeded the parent's opportunity to participate in decision-making about the provision of a FAPE;
- or caused a deprivation of educational benefit.

The file rarely records this, so it is not needed to recommend the claim. When the person chooses one of these claims, ask them in their own words what it affected, and put the answer in `statement.md`.
