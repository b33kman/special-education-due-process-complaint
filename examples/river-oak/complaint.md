## Preliminary statement

Petitioner Jordan Rivera (“Student”) is a student eligible for special education and related services under the category of Autism, and attends Willow Creek Elementary School in the River Oak Unified School District (“District”). This due process complaint is brought on Student’s behalf by the parent, Dana Rivera (“Parent”), who is self-represented.

The District has denied Student a free appropriate public education by carrying forward a reading fluency goal Student has never approached; by failing to evaluate Student’s reading after a written request; by declining to revise the goal when its own progress reports recorded that it was not met; and by failing to deliver the specialized academic instruction the operative individualized education program requires. Petitioner requests an impartial due process hearing on the problems stated in this complaint.

## Jurisdiction, timeliness and burden

A parent may file a due process complaint on any matter relating to the identification, evaluation or educational placement of a child with a disability, or the provision of a free appropriate public education to the child. 34 C.F.R. § 300.507(a)(1). The complaint must allege a violation that occurred not more than two years before the date the parent knew or should have known about the alleged action, unless the State has an explicit time limitation. 34 C.F.R. § 300.507(a)(2). The violations pleaded below begin with the individualized education program dated September 8, 2025 and continue to the date of this complaint.

Student resides in the District, which is the local educational agency responsible for making a free appropriate public education available to Student. 34 C.F.R. § 300.101.

## Statement of the problems

### A. Failure to provide a free appropriate public education

*34 C.F.R. §§ 300.101, 300.320, 300.324.*

An individualized education program must be reasonably calculated to enable a child to make progress appropriate in light of the child’s circumstances. *Endrew F. ex rel. Joseph F. v. Douglas Cnty. Sch. Dist. RE-1*, 580 U.S. 386, 399 (2017).

The goal the District adopted on September 8, 2025 was the goal from the previous program with its baseline rewritten, and Student began the year 65 words per minute below the end-of-second-grade benchmark on the same measure (paragraphs [#present] and [#goal]). Over three reporting periods the District recorded 21, 24 and 23 words per minute against a goal of 60, and its own March 6, 2026 report states that the goal was not met (paragraphs [#nov], [#jan] and [#mar]).

The District answered that the goal “remains appropriate” and that Student “is making progress toward it” (paragraph [#pwn]). Its own three measurements move four words per minute across five months and end below where the second of them stood, and the report that records the goal as not met recommends continuing it unchanged. The program the District has offered since September 8, 2025 has produced no meaningful progress toward the one reading goal it sets, and the District proposes to leave it in place until September 2026.

### B. Failure to evaluate appropriately and on time

*34 C.F.R. §§ 300.111, 300.301–300.311.*

A parent may request a reevaluation, and a public agency must conduct one when the parent requests it, subject to the limits in 34 C.F.R. § 300.303.

The Parent requested a reevaluation of Student’s reading in writing on December 2, 2025, naming phonological processing and decoding as the areas to assess (paragraph [#request]). The District replied two days later that it would prepare an assessment plan for her consent “within the required timeline” (paragraph [#promise]), repeated on March 12, 2026 that it “will provide an assessment plan” (paragraph [#plan]), and had provided none by April 2, 2026 (paragraph [#noresponse]).

The District has never refused the reevaluation. It accepted it twice in writing and did not begin it, so consent was never sought and no assessment has been conducted in the areas the Parent named. Student’s reading has been measured only by the fluency probe the disputed goal uses, and no assessment of decoding or phonological processing exists in the record from which an appropriate goal could be written.

### C. Failure to reconvene or revise the individualized education program

*34 C.F.R. § 300.324(b).*

The team must revise the individualized education program as appropriate to address any lack of expected progress toward the annual goals. 34 C.F.R. § 300.324(b)(1)(ii)(A).

The lack of expected progress was on the District’s own documents before the Parent asked for anything: three progress reports, the last recording the goal as not met (paragraphs [#nov], [#jan] and [#mar]). At the review on March 12, 2026 the team declined to revise the goal (paragraph [#declined]), and the Parent’s written request of March 14, 2026 drew no reply (paragraphs [#asked] and [#noresponse]).

The District’s stated reason is that it “will review the goal at the annual review in September 2026” (paragraph [#pwn]). That is the regular annual review the program already provided for, and it postpones any change until a full school year after the baseline was set. The obligation to revise arises from the lack of progress, not from the calendar.

### D. Failure to implement the services the individualized education program requires

*34 C.F.R. § 300.323.*

A public agency must make the special education and related services available to the child in accordance with the child’s individualized education program. 34 C.F.R. § 300.323(c)(2).

The program requires 240 minutes a week of specialized academic instruction in reading, in a small group of no more than 4 in the special education classroom (paragraph [#services]). The District’s own log records 20 of the 24 instructional weeks below that amount and an average of 95 minutes a week across the period (paragraph [#log]). The service stopped in the form the program specifies on October 6, 2025, when the reading intervention position fell vacant, and the position was still unfilled on February 27, 2026 (paragraphs [#block] and [#log]).

The District acknowledges the discontinuation and its cause, and records that “Compensatory services were not proposed at this meeting” (paragraph [#staffing]). A staffing vacancy is the District’s to solve. Across the 20 weeks the log records below the required amount, Student received 1,320 of the 4,800 minutes the program required, a shortfall of 3,480 minutes.

## Proposed resolution

*34 C.F.R. § 300.508(b)(6).*

The Parent proposes the following resolution:

(a) convene an individualized education program team meeting within 15 days to adopt measurable reading goals based on current assessment data (claims A and C; 34 C.F.R. § 300.324(b));

(b) fund an independent educational evaluation in reading and written expression at the District’s expense, including assessment of phonological processing and decoding (claim B; 34 C.F.R. § 300.502);

(c) provide 3,480 minutes of compensatory specialized academic instruction in reading, being the shortfall between the 4,800 minutes the individualized education program required in the 20 weeks the District’s service delivery log records below 240 minutes and the 1,320 minutes the log records as delivered (claim D; 34 C.F.R. § 300.323); and

(d) implement the individualized education program as written, in a small group of no more than 4 in the special education classroom, and provide the Parent the service delivery log each month (claim D; 34 C.F.R. § 300.323).
