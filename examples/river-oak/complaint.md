## Preliminary statement

Petitioner Jordan Rivera (“Student”) is a student eligible for special education and related services under the category of Autism, and attends Willow Creek Elementary School in the River Oak Unified School District (“District”). This due process complaint is brought on Student’s behalf by the parent, Dana Rivera (“Parent”), who is self-represented.

The District has denied Student a free appropriate public education by carrying forward a reading fluency rate Student has not moved toward; by failing to conduct the reevaluation of Student’s reading it twice undertook to begin; by declining to revise the goal when its own progress reports recorded that it was not met; and by failing to deliver the specialized academic instruction the operative individualized education program requires. Petitioner requests an impartial due process hearing on the problems stated in this complaint.

## Jurisdiction, timeliness and burden

A parent may file a due process complaint on any of the matters described in 34 C.F.R. § 300.503(a)(1) and (2): the identification, evaluation or educational placement of a child with a disability, or the provision of a free appropriate public education to the child. 34 C.F.R. § 300.507(a)(1). The complaint must allege a violation that occurred not more than two years before the date the parent knew or should have known about the alleged action, unless the State has an explicit time limitation. 34 C.F.R. § 300.507(a)(2). California sets a two-year period of its own in Education Code section 56505. The violations pleaded below begin with the individualized education program dated September 8, 2025 and continue to the date of this complaint.

Student resides in the District, which is the local educational agency responsible for making a free appropriate public education available to Student. 34 C.F.R. § 300.101.

## Statement of the problems

### A. Failure to provide a free appropriate public education

*34 C.F.R. §§ 300.101, 300.320, 300.324.*

An individualized education program must be reasonably calculated to enable a child to make progress appropriate in light of the child’s circumstances. *Endrew F. ex rel. Joseph F. v. Douglas Cnty. Sch. Dist. RE-1*, 580 U.S. 386, 399 (2017).

The goal the District adopted on September 8, 2025 was the goal from the previous program with its baseline rewritten, and the Parent took written exception to it at the meeting that adopted it (paragraphs [#goal] and [#exception]). Over three reporting periods the District recorded 21, 24 and 23 words per minute against a rate of 60, and its own March 6, 2026 report states that the goal was not met and recommends continuing it (paragraphs [#nov], [#jan] and [#mar]).

The District answered that the goal “remains appropriate” and that Student “is making progress toward it, as reflected in” those three reports (paragraph [#pwn]). The reports it relies on are the reports that record the rate falling between the second and the third. Student has met the accuracy criterion the goal sets and not the rate, which is the half of the goal the services address, and the District proposes no change to the rate, the services or the way progress is measured before September 2026.

### B. Failure to evaluate appropriately and on time

*34 C.F.R. §§ 300.303–300.311.*

A parent may request a reevaluation, and a public agency must conduct one when the parent requests it, subject to the limits in 34 C.F.R. § 300.303.

The Parent requested a reevaluation of Student’s reading in writing on December 2, 2025, naming phonological processing and decoding as the areas to assess (paragraph [#request]). The District replied two days later that it would prepare an assessment plan for her consent “within the required timeline” (paragraph [#promise]), repeated on March 12, 2026 that it “will provide an assessment plan” (paragraph [#plan]), and had provided none by April 2, 2026 (paragraph [#noresponse]).

The District has never refused the reevaluation. It accepted it twice in writing and did not begin it, so consent was never sought and no assessment has been conducted in either area the Parent named. The program’s own present levels describe Student’s decoding in words — vowel-team and silent-e patterns not yet applied — without any assessment of it, and no measure of phonological processing exists in the record from which services addressing it could be written (paragraph [#present]).

### C. Failure to reconvene or revise the individualized education program

*34 C.F.R. § 300.324(b).*

The team must revise the individualized education program as appropriate to address any lack of expected progress toward the annual goals. 34 C.F.R. § 300.324(b)(1)(ii)(A).

The lack of expected progress was on the District’s own documents before the Parent asked for anything: three progress reports, the last recording the goal as not met (paragraphs [#nov], [#jan] and [#mar]). At the review on March 12, 2026 the team declined to revise the goal (paragraph [#declined]), and the Parent’s written request of March 14, 2026 drew no reply (paragraphs [#asked] and [#noresponse]).

The District’s stated reason is that Student “is making progress toward” the goal, as reflected in those three reports (paragraph [#pwn]). Progress toward a rate is movement in the rate, and the rate the District recorded was lower in March than in January. A report that records a goal as not met and recommends continuing it unchanged is a reason to revise the program, not a reason to leave it until the annual review.

### D. Failure to implement the services the individualized education program requires

*34 C.F.R. § 300.323.*

A public agency must make the special education and related services available to the child in accordance with the child’s individualized education program. 34 C.F.R. § 300.323(c)(2).

The program requires 240 minutes a week of specialized academic instruction in reading, in a small group of no more than 4 in the special education classroom (paragraph [#services]). The District’s own log records 20 of the 24 instructional weeks below that amount and an average of 95 minutes a week across the period (paragraph [#log]). The service stopped in the form the program specifies on October 6, 2025, when the reading intervention position fell vacant, and the position was still unfilled on February 27, 2026 (paragraphs [#block] and [#log]).

The weeks the log attributes to the school calendar are not claimed here (paragraph [#calendar]). Across the weeks that remain, Student received 1,155 of the 3,600 minutes the program required, a shortfall of 2,445 minutes. The District acknowledges the discontinuation and its cause, and records that “Compensatory services were not proposed at this meeting” (paragraph [#staffing]). A staffing vacancy is the District’s to solve.

## Proposed resolution

*34 C.F.R. § 300.508(b)(6).*

The Parent proposes the following resolution:

(a) conduct the reevaluation of Student’s reading the Parent requested on December 2, 2025, assessing phonological processing and decoding (claim B; 34 C.F.R. §§ 300.303, 300.304);

(b) convene an individualized education program team meeting within 15 days of that assessment to adopt measurable reading goals based on it (claims A and C; 34 C.F.R. § 300.324(b));

(c) provide 2,445 minutes of compensatory specialized academic instruction in reading, being the shortfall between the 3,600 minutes the program required in the weeks the District’s service delivery log records below 240 minutes and does not attribute to the school calendar, and the 1,155 minutes the log records as delivered in those weeks (claim D; 34 C.F.R. § 300.323); and

(d) implement the individualized education program as written, in a small group of no more than 4 in the special education classroom, and provide the Parent the service delivery log each quarter (claim D; 34 C.F.R. § 300.323).
