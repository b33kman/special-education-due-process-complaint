### For the Parent to decide

- **The limitations cutoff is April 17, 2024**: two years before the planned filing date of April 17, 2026, under 34 C.F.R. § 300.507(a)(2) and California Education Code section 56505. Confirm both the date and the period before filing. The eligibility date and the prior program of March 11, 2024 are pleaded as background at paragraph 1 and fall before that cutoff; that program is not in the document set, and the allegation that the goal was carried forward from it rests on the 2025 program's own note. The violations pleaded begin with the program dated September 8, 2025. Whether an exception reaches anything earlier is not addressed here.
- **Which party bears the burden of proof in a California special education due process hearing was not confirmed in this session**, so the complaint pleads no allocation: a complaint does not have to, and asserting one unread is what the rule against guessing a subsection forbids. Find the provision in the California Education Code and read it before the hearing.
- **You asked for an independent educational evaluation at the District's expense.** That is not what remedy (a) asks for, and the reason matters: 34 C.F.R. § 300.502(b)(1) gives a right to an independent evaluation at public expense where the parent *disagrees with an evaluation the District obtained*. Claim B is that the District conducted none, so there is no District evaluation to disagree with and § 300.502 does not attach on these facts. Remedy (a) asks for the assessment the District twice said it would provide. If you would rather press for an independent evaluation, say so: the claim has to be pleaded differently.
- You asked for an evaluation in **written expression** as well as reading. No document in the file identifies a need in written expression, so it is neither pleaded nor asked for. If there is a document that does, add it and it can be.
- The 15 days in remedy (b) and the quarterly log in remedy (d) are your own choices, not figures from a document.
- 34 C.F.R. § 300.508(a)(2) requires a copy to the State educational agency. California's is the Department of Education, and the Office of Administrative Hearings conducts the hearing under its supervision, which is why OAH is the state recipient on the certificate. Confirm on the Department's own dispute-resolution page that it does not also want a copy sent directly to it.
